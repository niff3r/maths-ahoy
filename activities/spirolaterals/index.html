Spirolaterals

Walk 1, turn. Walk 2, turn. Walk 3, turn. Repeat. Will you ever get back home?

What students do

Students pick a short sequence of numbers, such as 1, 2, 3, and draw on grid paper: walk forward 1 unit and turn right, walk 2 and turn right, walk 3 and turn right, then start the sequence again. Some designs close up into beautiful patterns. Others drift away forever.

Then the questions start. Which sequences get back home? How many times does the sequence repeat before they do? What changes if you turn left sometimes, or use 60° or 120° turns on dot paper? Students test, predict, and try to explain what they see.

The maths behind it

Getting back home depends on the turns adding up to a full rotation, and on how the length of the sequence fits with the angle. Along the way, students meet angles, rotational symmetry, multiples, and lowest common multiples, all while making something worth putting on the wall.

Some spirolaterals to try

A few favourites, on different kinds of paper. Square grid paper suits 90° turns, and triangular dot paper suits 60° and 120° turns.
