Activities / Spirographs

Spirographs

A ring with 96 teeth, a wheel with 36. How many petals will the flower have?

What students do

Students draw spirograph flowers, rolling a toothed wheel around the inside of a toothed ring, and record the number of teeth on each alongside the number of petals they get.

Some pairs give lots of thin petals, others just a few fat ones. Students look for the pattern, make a conjecture, and test it by predicting the petal count before they draw. Can they choose a ring and wheel to get exactly 5 petals?

Keeping track

As they draw, students record the teeth on the ring and the wheel, how many petals the flower has, and how many times the wheel travels around the ring before the pen gets back to where it started.

The maths behind it

The pen keeps drawing until the wheel's teeth and the ring's teeth line up again, so the number of petals comes down to factors the two numbers share. It's a lovely, visual way into highest common factors and lowest common multiples.
