Dizzying squares

What do you get if you add up an infinite number of numbers?

What students do

Can you add up an infinite number of numbers and get a sensible answer? Students find out by colouring in. They draw a square inside a square inside a square, and shade one triangle from each layer to make a spiral. Each triangle is a fraction of the big square, so the spiral is an infinite sum. How much of the square does it cover?

The maths behind it

It seems impossible to add up numbers forever and get an answer. But if the numbers shrink fast enough, the total stops growing past a certain point. A sum that goes on forever is called an infinite series. In the spiral, each triangle is half the size of the one before, so the shaded area is the infinite series 1/8 + 1/16 + 1/32 + …, and it covers only part of the square.
